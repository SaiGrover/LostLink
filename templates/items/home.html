01

Something goes missing.

A wallet slips out in the library. Its owner fills out a tag: what it looks like, where, when, and a photo if they have one.

02

Someone finds it.

A day later, someone hands in a wallet from a desk nearby and tags it as found. A moderator reads both tags before they go on the wall.

03

LostLink recognises the pair.

Language and vision models compare the words and the photos, then weigh in the place, category, date, brand and colour. Both people get a dispatch with every reason shown.

04

A person verifies it.

The owner answers private questions only they could know. A moderator reads the answers and decides. The score is context, never the verdict.

05

It goes home.

Contact details unseal, the two meet, and a moderator confirms the handover. The tag is stamped returned and the case closes.

The matching engine

One score. Six threads.

Each signal is a thread between two tags; its thickness is what it adds to the score. If either tag has no photo, that thread isn't counted against the match: its weight is shared among the others.

{% icon 'shield' %}Human verification

A score is a lead, not a verdict.

Valuable things attract false claims. Every consequential step belongs to a person, and every decision is written down.

  1. Private questions

    The finder or a moderator asks what only the owner could know. Answers are visible to the people involved, nobody else.

    Asked

  2. A moderator decides

    Answers are required before approval. Moderators can't approve their own claims, and only one owner can ever be verified per item.

    Approved

  3. Contacts unseal

    Phone numbers and emails stay hidden until approval, then appear to both people so they can meet.

    Unsealed

  4. Handover, on record

    A moderator confirms the physical return. Approvals, rejections, disputes and handovers all land in the decision log.

    Returned
