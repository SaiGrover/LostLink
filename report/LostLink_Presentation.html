The problem

Campus recovery today is scattered and unsafe

  • Scattered: class groups, notice boards and the security desk never meet in one place.
  • Unsearchable: posts get buried; no filtering by place, date or category.
  • Inconsistent: the same wallet is described in different words; photos are often missing.
  • Unsafe: phone numbers posted publicly, and anyone can say “that's mine”.
  • Unaccountable: nobody records who handed what to whom.

Our question

Can multimodal AI similarity narrow a noisy search while keeping people in control of who actually gets the item?

The machine proposes a lead. A moderator verifies ownership. The owner's one-time code confirms the return.

AI matching engine · synopsis-v3

Six signals, weighted and explained

SignalWeight
Title + description0.30
Photograph (ResNet-50)0.30
Campus zone0.15
Category0.10
Date and time0.10
Brand / colour0.05

Score

Weighted sum over the signals with evidence; missing ones are dropped and the rest re-weighted. The share actually compared is shown as evidence coverage.

Heuristic tiers

Strong > 0.85 Moderate 0.70–0.85 Weak < 0.70
Documented thresholds, not probabilities. Low coverage or suspicious dates cap the tier.

Never an approval

A score ranks leads for people to check. It is not proof of ownership.

What we improved

Making the scores stable and honest

Stable text model

Old TF-IDF changed a pair's score whenever unrelated reports were added. Now a fixed, versioned vocabulary over title + description, with synonyms:

“Black mobile phone with cracked screen” vs “Dark cellphone with damaged display” → 0.95

Calibrated images

ResNet gives unrelated photos a cosine of ~0.275, never 0. We rescale above that chance level:

s = (cos − 0.275) / (1 − 0.275)

Colours as sets

“navy blue” = “blue” (1.0) but “red and blue” vs “red” is only 0.67 — partial, not full agreement.

Graded chronology

Found-before-lost pairs are no longer silently hidden: plausible approximate suspicious contradictory — penalised and flagged for moderators.

Ownership workflow

Verification by people, protected by the database

  • Evidence checklist: the moderator records statement, private details, answers and evidence; approval is refused if any check contradicts the claim.
  • Reservation: an approved claim reserves the item — one per item, enforced by a database constraint.
  • Disputes are separate records: they pause the handover but never release the item to someone else.
  • Reconsideration: a rejected claimant can appeal with new evidence (limited, with full history).
  • Independence: nobody reviews their own report or decides their own case.
  LostLinkOwnership workflow
One-time-code handover

Only the owner's code returns an item

1. Owner creates a 6-digit code at the meeting — shown once.

2. After receiving and checking the item, the owner tells the code; the finder enters it.

Safeguards

  • Python secrets; stored only as salted HMAC-SHA-256
  • 10-minute expiry, single use
  • Locked after 5 wrong tries; rate limited
  • Paused by disputes
  • Moderators see status, never the code

Security and privacy

Every rule is enforced on the server

Campus identity

Email verification, campus-domain policy, password reset, rate-limited sign-in.

Private evidence

Expected answers and private details hidden from claimants and the public.

Safe photos

Re-encoded on upload (EXIF/GPS removed); ID cards blurred for others.

Audit trail

Append-only decision log with previous and new state.

Web security

CSRF, HSTS, secure cookies, deny-by-default media, CSV-injection guard.

Safe production

Refuses weak secrets, wildcard hosts or a missing campus domain.

Honest limitations and next steps

What we can and cannot claim

We can say

  • The full workflow works end to end and is tested.
  • Scores are stable, explained and versioned.
  • Ownership and handover cannot be bypassed.
  • Privacy and campus identity are enforced.

We cannot yet say

  • How accurate matching is on real campus reports.
  • That thresholds are calibrated — they are documented heuristics.
